# Technical notes

How GPSLess works under the hood, what it has been measured to do, and the data formats and tools behind it. For using the app see the [README](../README.md) and the [user guide](USER_GUIDE.md).

## Positioning engine

The phone requests Core Motion device data at 100 Hz with `xArbitraryZVertical`. `VehicleMotionProcessor` owns the shared live/simulation/raw-replay input path. At the user-confirmed parked start it averages total acceleration to establish gravity and measures residual gyro offsets over four seconds. Since engine 1.9, gyro calibration estimates uncertainty from fixed 250 ms blocks in vehicle right/up/forward axes. Core Motion already corrects device-motion gyro bias; an additional offset is applied only when its magnitude exceeds twice the estimated standard error. This prevents a noisy finite-window average from becoming a permanent gravity-rotation correction, while retaining supported residual offsets. While driving, it propagates that gravity vector using calibrated three-axis rotation and removes slow drift with the vibration speed filter described below. It reconstructs synchronized total acceleration from Core Motion's `userAcceleration + gravity`, removes the propagated gravity, then projects translation and yaw into the mounted vehicle axes. The separately recorded raw accelerometer and gyro remain available for cross-checks. Forward, lateral and vertical acceleration and yaw are averaged with sensor-time weights to approximately 20 Hz. UI estimates update at roughly 5 Hz.

Engine 3.0 corrects gravity drift without Core Motion's gravity, which field drives showed carries a forward bias near 0.1 m/s². Gravity is observed as total acceleration plus the vehicle's own acceleration. While moving, that vehicle acceleration is the processed forward acceleration minus the vibration filter's bias estimate, plus centripetal speed × yaw; the correction blends in over 20 seconds, scaled by speed confidence. Using the filter's rate of speed change instead would be circular without an axle echo: a lagging speed absorbs a sustained acceleration into gravity. When parked vibration confirms a stop, vehicle acceleration is zero and the blend takes 2 seconds, rejected when total acceleration departs from gravity by more than 3° so a departure is never absorbed. Each correction's effect on processed forward acceleration is handed back to the filter's bias state. On five GPS-referenced drives the open-loop propagation of engine 2.1 reached 3–9° of gravity disagreement and −0.2 to −0.5 m/s² of forward-acceleration bias within five minutes; its Core Motion correction had fired 0% of the time. Explicit settled stops still re-establish gravity and gyro offsets. Gravity-direction disagreement with Core Motion, correction size and speed-aid weight are logged for inspection, not presented as measured attitude accuracy.

### Vibration speed (engine 3.0)

Phone acceleration integrates to speed only for a minute or two: a 0.1 m/s² residual bias becomes 6 m/s after a minute, which is why earlier engines reported 0 km/h while the car cruised at 50–60 km/h. Engine 3.0 measures speed instead. Every road bump reaches the front axle first and the rear axle one wheelbase later, so the mounted phone feels the same disturbance repeated after wheelbase ÷ speed. Seven GPS-referenced Kyiv drives showed this echo at the same delay, matching the test car's wheelbase within 2%, in eleven device-axis channel pairs, each with a consistent sign.

`VehicleSpeedObserver` resamples device motion to a uniform 100 Hz grid and, every 0.5 seconds, correlates the last 4 seconds of whitened 1–40 Hz vibration across those channel pairs. A grid filter over speed (0–40 m/s) and residual acceleration bias propagates with integrated forward acceleration and weighs each hypothesis by the echo correlation at its implied delay. The 4-second window measures speed at its centre, so each hypothesis is compared using the speed change the accelerometer measured since then. Broadband 3–45 Hz vibration relative to the four-second parked calibration separates rolling from parked; on field drives parked vibration stayed below 0.32 log₁₀ decades above that reference and every moving interval above 0.57. The parked likelihood ratio is capped near 10:1. A window without measurable vibration energy, or a calibration on a numerically silent sensor, provides no evidence and disables the observer.

Engine 3.3 extends the echo to highway speed. Delays shorter than 0.1 s were ignored, which for a 2.82 m wheelbase is 101 km/h: faster driving was pulled towards that edge, and the gravity correction, aided by the low speed, dragged it further. On a two-hour, 127 km highway drive vibration speed read a median 14 km/h low at 105–115 km/h and 45 km/h low above, down to 60–80 km/h low in the worst tenth, and the position ran up to 9 km ahead or behind. The echo itself stayed at its expected delay up to 125 km/h, and correlation noise at 60–90 ms delays was within 25% of that at 100–150 ms, so delays down to 0.06 s now count. The correlation is evaluated at 2.5 ms steps by a four-times longer inverse transform of the same spectrum, because at 7–10 samples one 10 ms step is 10–14% of speed. Above 90 km/h the echo weakens (median peak 2.2σ at 105–115 km/h against 3.5σ in town) while repeating vibration from the wheels and drivetrain keeps false peaks near 0.67 and 1.35 times the speed at about 3.5σ; removing its spectral lines or whitening more strongly also removed echo energy and made every speed worse. Instead the echo's weight falls linearly from full at 90 km/h to 45% at 115 km/h, for all speed hypotheses alike, so the filter relies more on integrated acceleration there, and the grid stops at 144 km/h, where a 45 m/s grid had let a false peak hold 158 km/h at 113 km/h for a minute. On the highway drive, up to where it left the route, the worst tenth of vibration speed at 105–115 km/h is now 9 km/h low (was 65) and above 115 km/h 15 km/h low (was 79), with medians within 3 km/h; the median along-route error fell from 42 to 30 m, the 90th percentile from 1,040 to 160 m and the worst from 8.9 km to 560 m, and the reported uncertainty covered the GPS error 96% of the time instead of 79%. City drives were unchanged within a few metres, except the 90th percentile of one 1 km drive (25 to 48 m).

Engine 3.4 rejects repeating vibration. Averaged over 1,534 steady-speed windows at 35–65 km/h, the residual correlation showed the axle echo at its delay and a second comb at 0.785, 1.57 and 2.36 times it: tyre non-uniformity repeating once per wheel revolution, whose period is a fixed fraction of the axle delay (here a 2.24 m circumference on a 2.85 m wheelbase). Its first tooth held vibration speed 1.2–1.36 times too high for up to a minute at 45–60 km/h, confidently (±1.5–2 km/h), and caused most of the city position error beyond 100 m; the second tooth is the 0.67× highway mode above. A single echo does not repeat, so the evidence at each delay is now reduced by twice the mean positive correlation at two and three times that delay. Below 20 km/h the echo is rarely measurable (the strongest peak lay within 8% of the true delay in 10% of windows, close to chance), while short-delay correlation in slow turns supported speeds three to six times too high; the echo's weight falls to 40% below 11 km/h, rising to full at 20 km/h, based on the predicted mean speed. In one slow 110° turn the filter briefly held two modes and reported their mean, 75 ± 36 km/h; the engine's fixed 10% pull toward the measured speed carried its estimate from 30 to 110 km/h, leaving the position 150 m ahead for three minutes along a straight road. The pull now falls with the square of the measured spread beyond 1 m/s. Using the comb itself as speed evidence (it is a wheel-speed signal once the circumference is known) lowered speed errors further but needs a learned circumference and, applied at short delays, supported false high speeds when driving slowly; it is not used. On 11 GPS-referenced drives (about 3 hours, including the highway drive) the share of moving time with vibration-derived speed more than 10 km/h off fell from 9.2% to 6.8% and more than 20 km/h off from 2.2% to 1.6%; the drive with the slow turn went from a 60 m median and 248 m worst error to 34 m and 126 m, and the reported uncertainty's worst per-drive coverage of the GPS error rose from 53% to 72%. One slow 7-minute drive's 90th percentile rose from 41 to 63 m.

**Uncertainty shown to the driver.** The engine's uncertainty is an outer radius: across these drives the GPS error stayed within it 95% of moving time, and half of it held the error 84% of the time. Growing it more slowly (a 30- or 15-second instead of 60-second speed-error correlation) dropped pooled coverage to 85% and 78%, one drive to 13%, because the errors are heavy-tailed: usually small, sometimes 150 m while a false speed persists. Route matching and resets keep the outer radius. The app shows the half radius as **±** and on the map, which reads like a phone's GPS accuracy (a 68% radius), and reports "Position uncertain" above an outer radius of 90 m instead of 45 m; at 45 m the status showed for 92% of moving time while the error exceeded 50 m in only 22%. The median drive now shows it for 47% of moving time instead of 89%; the error exceeded 50 m in 21% of moving time, against 29% before this engine.

**Automatic wheelbase refinement.** A wrong wheelbase scales every vibration distance by one factor, while the mapped distance between two matched route turns is known. Between consecutive route-evidence matches anchored at turn midpoints (at least 150 m and 15 minutes apart, without a gap in vibration speed), the ratio of mapped to vibration distance gives one wheelbase estimate. Its uncertainty combines both anchors (5 m or 10% of the feature length; U-turns and loops 10 m or 25%) with 2.5% vibration-distance noise; ratios outside 0.8–1.25 are treated as wrong matches. Estimates are pooled by inverse variance across drives and stored on the phone. Once at least two intervals give ±1.5% or better, the engine scales vibration speed by the learned value immediately and the setting is updated to it (rounded to 1 cm) for later drives. Changing the value by hand restarts learning; switching refinement off keeps the configured value but still records `wheelbase-calibration` rows. Replaying eight field drives with the setting started 7% low, at the true value and 7% high, learning converged within 0.7% of the wheelbase fitted with GPS speed (±1.4–1.5%, 12–13 intervals). A changed wheelbase keeps the parked calibration.

The engine reweights its hypotheses by the measured speed (tempered, with a small pull that weakens as the measurement spreads), which also selects consistent acceleration biases. One second of confident parked vibration stops the engine even without observed braking; two consecutive moving observations release a stop while creeping too gently for the acceleration impulse. Position uncertainty grows with the integrated speed variance (60-second error correlation) instead of t². Observations are recorded as `vibration-speed` rows and used by ordinary replay; raw replay recomputes them.

On five GPS-referenced drives, raw replay of engine 3.0 had median along-route errors of 4–30 m and worst errors of 30–104 m over 0.9–3.9 km, against 24–945 m and 56–2,959 m for engine 2.1; speed error was 1.5–3.0 km/h against 4.9–38 km/h. The vibration filter alone, without the map, accumulated 0.6–2.5% distance error. GPS speed lagged the accelerometer by 0.4–0.85 s on these drives and was compared with that allowance. These drives came from one car, phone and rigid mount, and the parameters were chosen on them; other cars, softer mounts, very smooth roads and long traffic jams need their own validation. Below about 4 km/h the echo delay exceeds the 2.5-second analysis range, so creeping relies on acceleration and the parked/rolling evidence. Periodic road structure can still lock the echo to a multiple of the true delay.

The bounded, deterministic particle filter tracks directed road edge, offset along the edge, velocity, heading, accelerometer bias, gyro bias and probability. It branches only onto connected roads, observes mapped one-way directions and supported node-based turn restrictions, and scores:

- Integrated forward acceleration and heading change.
- Road tangent agreement, with additional tolerance through intersections.
- Sustained heading change versus continuous road geometry, including gentle bends and alternating curves across short map segments.
- Lateral acceleration divided by yaw rate as a noisy speed observation during suitable turns, requiring a continuous mapped bend in the same direction; lane-change yaw on a mapped straight supplies no such speed cue.
- Recent completed turns, the actual change in road heading, branch probability and spatial spread before counting a turn match. An observed turn alone cannot become an anchor on a straight road.
- Distinct timed sharp junctions, with joint position, speed and accelerometer-bias correction and bounded raw-acceleration integration between successive trusted junctions.
- Measured U-turns onto an available opposite direction of the same road when replaying legacy sessions without a selected route. A locked route never switches to an unselected reverse edge.
- Road vibration, pitch/roll changes and gravity quality in the motion model. A constant mounting angle does not add velocity noise.

Displayed position and speed use the same continuous directed road-path hypothesis. Paths join legal, uniquely aligned continuations with less than 30 degrees of boundary heading change, including across OSM way identifiers. Distances are averaged along the whole path and then converted back to an edge and offset. Competing shallow forks, ambiguous merges, disconnected parallel roads and closed cycles remain separate. Crossing an arbitrary edge boundary therefore does not discard forward candidates from the displayed position. At junctions, candidate mass is distributed over legal exits according to inertial heading with a 0.35-radian angular scale; passing a side road no longer divides a straight-moving candidate equally among every exit.

Since engine 1.6, tangent measurements span the continuous path instead of stopping at every edge seam. Curvature likelihoods compare each candidate's actual heading and map-heading changes over consecutive, non-overlapping six-second intervals. Changing speed, curve entry/exit and successive opposite bends therefore retain their temporal order. No curvature score is applied on a straight mapped interval, or across ambiguous path boundaries. A segment transition alone is never an observed landmark.

The curvature likelihood allows temporary heading offsets caused by lane changes, with a speed-dependent tolerance based on a 2.2 m/s lateral component. That covers the peak of the synthetic smooth 3.5 m, three-second lane changes used in regression checks; it is not a guaranteed physical bound. Longer windows and this tolerance reduce the influence of short steering reversals, especially at low speed. The engine cannot classify every lane change from IMU data and a road centre line. Lane changes during a bend, repeated road shapes and inaccurate geometry can remain ambiguous. Curvature is tempered, correlated evidence that reweights existing hypotheses; it does not increment the turn-anchor count or reset accumulated uncertainty. Constant curvature alone does not locate a unique point along an arc.

Since engine 1.7, an isolated mapped 45–120-degree turn can provide a stronger, timed junction observation. It must be predominantly one-directional, last 0.7–12 seconds, agree with a unique legal junction between straight approaches, and have at least 95% candidate mass on its outgoing path. The sensor angle range includes the same 0.20-radian tolerance used to compare it with the map; a measured 123-degree turn is no longer rejected before comparison with a compatible mapped 115-degree turn. Repeated matching junctions in the search interval and compound turns remain excluded. The angular midpoint is an approximate road-location observation with at least 12 m standard deviation, increased for the turn's duration and speed. This allowance covers uncertain lane choice and corner cutting; the midpoint is not asserted to be the exact mapped vertex.

Engine 2.0 requires a locked route in the live app. Offline planning uses directed edges and supported turn restrictions. The initial heading is preserved; either legal arrival direction at point B is considered. Propagation follows only the selected edge sequence, and an unexpected turn can still cause heading-mismatch reset. Route knowledge does not authorize automatic speed or position resets at an expected distance. Legacy route-free recordings remain replayable.

Engine 2.1 builds held fast hypotheses before an expected route bend until compatible yaw began. That gate was superseded by route-wide evidence matching and was no longer called in later builds that still reported the same engine version; engine 3.0 removes it. Recordings with `route-constraint` rows remain readable.

Engine 3.1 restores a simpler bend gate, because a cloud that runs ahead of the car otherwise carried the dot past a bend before the car reached it: every particle was equally wrong, so reweighting could not pull them back. A particle whose gyro heading still matches its current road does not advance onto road pointing 35° or more away from that heading; it waits until the turn shows on the gyro. The hold is shared by the estimate and capped at 120 m of driving, after which the gate opens for everyone for 80 m, so a map error or a missed turn cannot trap the estimate. On replayed field drives it lowered the median error of one Kyiv drive from 23 to 15 m (worst 49 to 32 m) and changed no drive for the worse by more than 3 m.

Route-wide evidence (`RouteEvidenceMatcher`) lists every directional feature of the locked route: junction turns, rounded bends and adjacent pairs forming compound turns of 20° or more. Engine 3.0 scored each completed gyro turn alone, with a position prior four times the displayed uncertainty and a floor for any feature ahead. On curvy mountain roads every bend within kilometres then looked plausible: all 35 turns of one 13.5 km field drive stayed ambiguous at 13–23% confidence, so nothing reduced the uncertainty, which grew to 350 m and stopped tracking while the real error was 27 m.

Engine 3.1 tracks the route position with up to 32 weighted hypotheses, each a route offset and variance advanced by the odometer (6% of the distance driven, plus a 5% share allowing 30% for the axle echo locking onto evenly spaced road joints). Each completed turn of 30° or more branches every hypothesis into one child per nearby feature, Kalman-updated to that feature's mapped midpoint (σ 8 m or 30% of its length) and weighted by angle (σ 12° + 15% of the angle) and position agreement, plus one child for a turn the map does not explain, whose weight falls with the turn angle. A small hypothesis around the engine estimate with its own conservative uncertainty is added before each turn, so a wrong sequence can be abandoned. One bend rarely decides anything on a mountain road, but the measured spacing of the next bends quickly eliminates wrong alignments. A feature is accepted once hypotheses explaining the turn by it hold 75% of the weight; accepted features bind nothing, since every hypothesis keeps its own sequence. The accepted anchor is their weighted mean and spread; weaker alternatives widen the reported uncertainty. On four mountain-road drives and five GPS-referenced city drives, replay changed the median along-route error from 60 to 37 m on the 13.5 km drive and was equal or better elsewhere, while the median reported uncertainty fell from 262 to 114 m (Kyiv 69–158 m to 52–141 m), still covering the GPS error 89–100% of the time. Two drives whose starting point was tapped 180–215 m from where the car actually was remain wrong: the first turn fitted a bend near the tapped point, and one drive ended before a second turn could correct it. A locked route stops tracking only above 10 km of uncertainty since engine 3.1.1 (350 m without a route): a later turn can restore the position, and on a highway the driver cannot stop to set it again. Route lookups use a precomputed index, so building route features for a 660 km route takes 0.1 s instead of 91 s on a Mac.

Engine 3.2 matches heading profiles instead of discrete features. On a 25-minute Lviv drive the road bent in long sweeps (−101° over 730 m, +69° over 690 m) and S-bends that the gyro reports as one turn, while the feature list keeps only bends shorter than 220 m, in pieces; 38 of 46 turns stayed unmatched, the dot ran up to 280 m ahead and the uncertainty reached 600 m, although every one of those turns was on the map within 5° of the angle the gyro measured. Now each completed gyro turn of 15° or more (was 30°) yields its heading profile: yaw integrated against the odometer from 40 m before the turn until now, up to 2 km, sampled at most 80 times. The route's heading is tabulated every 5 m from a 20 m chord. For every 5 m position of the car along the route the matcher compares both after removing the constant heading offset, at odometer scales from 0.8 to 1.25, with 5° per 40 m of profile. Each hypothesis multiplies its prior by that fit; every separate peak (at most three) becomes a child, which counts as matched only where the route bends by at least half the observed angle in the same direction. A turn fitting nowhere better than 9° RMS is treated as unmapped, less readily the sharper it is. A turn is accepted when 75% of the weight matched a bend at one place and the fit there is within 14° RMS: true places fitted within 1–7°, a wide arc through a sharply drawn corner 11°, service roads missing from the map 22°. The discrete features remain for wheelbase calibration and logging. After a match the uncertainty is the anchor's 2σ, widened by as much as alternatives widen the radius holding 95% of all hypotheses; the mixture's standard deviation used before let 2% of far-away fallback weight hold a 98%-confident match at 300 m. A confident anchor also narrows the particles' spread along the route to its own. On replay the 25-minute drive's median error fell from 129 to 25 m (90th percentile 255 to 111 m) and its median uncertainty from 381 to 92 m, covering the GPS error 90% of the time instead of 77%; the 13.5 km mountain drive went from 37 to 13 m and the Kyiv drives from 47 to 40 m and 20 to 13 m. Two short drives whose first minute was on service roads missing from the map, where the phone's GPS itself was 85–127 m off the route, remain wrong until their first mapped junction. Separately, a stop correction no longer adds the motion-uncertainty growth twice, which made the radius jump whenever the car stopped.

Since engine 3.0 an accepted match anchors the turn's angular midpoint to the feature's mapped midpoint and adds the distance driven since. The gyro declares a turn complete only after 0.8 s below 1.4°/s, 20–90 m past the mapped bend, so the earlier end anchor placed the car about 20 m behind on average and up to 91 m. Midpoints agreed within 5 m for ordinary turns and 44 m for one U-turn loop. The anchor (σ = 8 m or 30% of the feature length) is fused with the estimate by a Kalman gain from the modeled uncertainty; only an estimate inconsistent with its own uncertainty is moved fully to the anchor. With vibration speed available, the route-average speed override is skipped because the interval average includes any stop between the turns. Route-evidence events record the anchor, its σ, the anchor type and whether the correction was `kalman` or `recovery_shift`. Since engine 3.3 a recovery shift also needs the matcher's 95% radius around the anchor to be less than half the shift, so that the current position lies outside it; otherwise the turn is logged as ambiguous and later turns decide. After 11 minutes of highway without a match, a 47° turn where the car left the route fitted a bend 10 km back with 90% of the weight, the rest staying at the correct estimate; moving there raised the uncertainty past 10 km and stopped tracking. The one recovery shift on the city drives, a 235 m snap-back with a 10 m radius, is unaffected. A U-turn of 150° or more that fits nowhere used to stop tracking as leaving the route even where the route itself turns round, because a reversal can be driven either way round (a left loop, a right-hand loop through a car park, a three-point turn) and its heading profile then differs from the mapped one: on one drive the car reversed with a single 183° right-hand sweep where the map draws a 189° left loop, on another the fit missed the 14° limit by 3°. Since engine 3.3 such a U-turn is anchored to the end of the nearest route reversal (150° within 150 m) within 150 m or 1.5 times the uncertainty, whichever is larger, with σ 25 m, and the matcher restarts from there; without a mapped reversal nearby it still stops tracking.

For selected routes, timed landmarks come from the complete route geometry across graph seams, including isolated rounded bends inside an edge. Tangents sampled every 5 m over a 10 m span identify predominantly single-direction 45–120-degree bends with 20 m of quiet geometry separating turns and a bend span no longer than 100 m. The mapped angular midpoint supplies the approximate anchor. U-turns, compound bends, ambiguous repeated turns and incomplete approach/exit geometry do not become precise anchors just because the route is known. Joint corrections must remain on the route and agree with the measured heading. Distance calibration between accepted landmarks uses their cumulative route offsets, even across continuous-path boundaries. The route is written once in the recording header; accepted corrections include route distance, timing, position/speed/bias changes and uncertainty. GPS remains outside this calculation.

The first such junction jointly conditions position, velocity and bias. Successive trusted junctions on the same continuous road, 10–180 seconds and at least 80 m apart, also constrain integrated distance. Two raw-acceleration integrals retain measurements that intervening map reweighting cannot rewrite. Integrating backward from each live speed/bias hypothesis predicts distance between the timed junctions; comparison with mapped distance conditions the same joint ensemble. The update retains information acquired from intervening geometry instead of replacing it with a separate speed estimate. Measurement error includes both junctions and a conservative allowance for changing bias and velocity noise. Stops, resets and U-turns invalidate the calibration interval. Sensor history for turn timing is kept for 150 seconds (60 before engine 3.2), long enough for the slowest sweeping bends; no extra raw sampling or continuous recording stream is added. On five field drives the timed correction applied twice in 18 accepted turns, with negligible change: most real turns last 7–39 seconds on the gyroscope because yaw decays slowly after the corner, beyond its 12-second limit. Route-wide midpoint anchoring and vibration speed now carry that role.

Candidate populations are resampled with a seeded generator and bounded both across the road network and within a transition. Initial hypotheses balance position, heading and bias signs; propagation and resampling perturbations have a zero weighted mean while preserving their expected variance. An uncertainty floor grows with motion time even when particles cluster, and stopping does not erase it: with vibration speed it is twice the square root of 60 s × ∫σ²dt of the measured speed, otherwise 0.004 m/s² × t². An angle match alone retains the preceding published uncertainty and does not restart the motion clock. A successful timed junction observation may restart uncertainty propagation from its position allowance and remaining speed/bias covariance, with residual model-error and process-noise floors. The 350 m reset threshold is unchanged. On a locked route the reset threshold is 10 km since engine 3.1.1. These are modeled uncertainty estimates, not calibrated accuracy guarantees. Recovery after the correct branch has disappeared, featureless long straights, inaccurate geometry and ambiguous exits remain limitations.

Departure uses accumulated forward velocity change over up to four seconds while waiting to move. It requires both a positive impulse above 0.15 m/s and mean forward acceleration of at least 0.08 m/s². This blocks the observed quiet 0.04–0.05 m/s² drift from releasing the stopped marker. Very weak creeping can consequently remain classified as stopped until clearer acceleration occurs; this threshold is not an independent speed measurement. Departure impulse and acceleration support are included in stop diagnostics. Once moving, the history returns to two seconds for recent braking evidence. The accepted departure includes its buffered movement and velocity. Brief cancelling acceleration pulses remain stationary. Braking also uses accumulated negative velocity change, allowing gentle deceleration to be recognized.

Automatic stopping requires recent braking, settled statistics over the motion window, and at least half the hypothesis probability below **3 km/h**. This deliberately treats very slow movement as stopped. Window means, standard deviation and RMS tolerate stationary engine vibration; individual spikes no longer repeatedly restart the quiet check. Near-zero probability avoids the positive average caused by clipping uncertain negative speeds to zero. Quiet cruising alone never proves a stop, and slowing to approximately 5 km/h does not force zero. If the whole speed population remains too high at a real stop, use the explicit stop action; phone-only IMU data cannot resolve every creep/coast/stop ambiguity. Stop evidence and rejected gates are recorded. Road observations may update moving bias hypotheses. Particle speeds remain nonnegative; braking overshoot does not prove reverse gear.

When automatic confirmation is delayed, the engine can remove movement accumulated since the latest supported low-speed interval. It retains at most eight seconds / 64 published history points and rewinds at most five seconds / five metres, with a tighter bound based on the 3 km/h policy. Every hypothesis must remain on the same continuous road, without crossing a path boundary or accepted turn correction. The correction preserves uncertainty and does not count as a road anchor. This is a conservative policy estimate, not a measurement of the exact physical stopping point. An event records the inferred onset, before/after road positions, removed distance, and applied/skipped reason. Explicit stop confirmation does not rewind position.

Unexpected pitch/roll rates, extreme yaw, sensor gaps, lost map agreement and leaving the coverage area stop the estimator and require recovery. Wrapped angles are compared by their shortest difference. Each stop has a specific message and a structured `failure` reason in the recorded estimate, including the measured value and threshold when applicable. These checks cannot detect every phone movement, nor reliably distinguish every vehicle stop from coasting. Reverse gear is not detected or tracked; if you reverse, pause and set the current road position again before forward driving. Use the pause button and maintain the supported mount.

Magnetometer magnitude and relative barometric altitude are shown in diagnostics. Full magnetic vectors and barometric pressure are also recorded. They are not absolute position observations, and the magnetic compass is not used to force heading inside a car. Raw attitude quaternion, gravity, acceleration and rotation are retained for investigating mount and gravity-compensation errors.

MapLibre receives a disabled location provider and `showsUserLocation = false`; the app supplies only an ordinary custom annotation. Its map styles, geometry and label glyphs use local file URLs. The optional GPS reference service has its own Core Location subscription and cannot move the marker or initialize/correct the positioning engine.

The globe button below the header scales Apple's online MapKit imagery over the current offline map. Press the map button in the same place to scale it away. Selection controls remain on screen, so you can tap a road, drag the marker along the offline graph and confirm direction without entering another screen. The green line is the selected graph segment; imagery and road geometry can differ. MapKit has user location and user tracking disabled. Satellite imagery is not downloaded for offline use; Apple's attribution remains visible.

The device-to-vehicle conversion uses `-9.80665 * userAcceleration` before projection: Core Motion acceleration has the opposite sign to physical translation. Gyroscope signs retain the right-hand rule, then convert to clockwise-positive vehicle yaw. A rear-facing screen and forward departure must produce positive forward acceleration. Raw recordings preserve Apple's original values; headers identify the processed convention as `coremotion-negated-g-v1`. This was corrected in engine 1.3 / app 0.1.2 after three recorded forward departures exposed reversed acceleration. The sign conversion is also used for Core Motion accelerometer input by the [HKUST VINS-Mobile implementation](https://github.com/HKUST-Aerial-Robotics/VINS-Mobile/blob/master/VINS_ios/ViewController.mm#L1048).

## Offline data

Two regions are bundled; one is loaded at a time and the **Map** choice (on the first card or in Settings) switches between them while no drive is active. Switching resets the selection and route. Recordings are prefixed with their region (`Kyiv_…`, `Lviv_…`) and record `mapRegion`; replay asks you to switch region for a drive recorded on the other map. The loaded graph also sets the plane in which road geometry is measured: Kyiv keeps its original equirectangular plane, so its snapshot and recordings are unchanged; the Lviv region uses a stereographic plane centred on the region, whose scale error stays below 0.01% (the Kyiv plane would be about 3% short east-west in the Carpathians).

**Lviv region** (snapshot **2026-09-25T01:41:46Z**, `lviv-*` files) contains all of Lviv Oblast with the Kyiv road classes, plus two additions around Slavske. Within 48.60–49.05 N, 23.00–23.80 E it also includes unpaved `track` roads (except `tracktype=grade5`), because many village access roads there are tagged that way; tracks are drawn dashed and cost three times their length in route planning. Zakarpattia land within 48.55–49.10 N, 22.95–23.85 E is included so routes over the Beskyd and Veretsky passes (Volovets, Pylypets, Nyzhni Vorota) do not stop at the oblast line. It has 105,930 road ways (4,430 tracks), 381,458 directed edges, 25,906 display areas and 1,079 node restrictions; the graph is 95 MB and takes about twice as long as Kyiv to load. It is built by `tools/build_lviv.py` from the openstreetmap.fr `lviv_oblast` and `zakarpattia_oblast` extracts (download them into `data-source/`; the script needs `pip install osmium`), and `lviv-manifest.json` records their SHA-256. Lviv's historic centre is largely pedestrian, so the nearest car road can be over 150 m from Rynok Square.

**Kyiv**: the included OSM snapshot is **2026-09-09T21:11:01Z**, covering the Kyiv rectangle **50.21–50.64 N, 30.23–30.83 E**. It contains 74,830 selectable motor-road ways, 255,980 directed edges, 13,332 display areas and 4,376 node-based restrictions. The orange outline marks the coverage boundary. Geometric crossings are connected only when OSM node topology connects them; a bridge crossing another road is not itself a junction.

The graph uses full road centre-line geometry. It does not provide lane boundaries, live closures, parking-garage maps, or lane-level accuracy. The extract contains 107 conditional or via-way restrictions that are not evaluated by this prototype; their count is recorded in the manifest. One approach at OSM node 372861281 has contradictory mandatory turns. When conflicting rules eliminate all tracking candidates, the app records `map_restriction_conflict` with edge, way and junction identifiers instead of calling it a road ending. The real-world restriction needs verification; the app does not guess which rule to discard. Route previews reflect only the supported restrictions in this snapshot; no spoken directions are offered. Some water/park multipolygons are incomplete because the display currently uses closed OSM ways. Roads, rather than buildings, are the primary visual reference.

Map labels (`kyiv-pois.geojson`, 24,379 labels; `lviv-pois.geojson`, 27,788) are built by `tools/build_pois.py kyiv|lviv` from the same extracts: settlements, districts, named peaks with their height, railway stations, fuel, shops, food, lodging, health, landmarks (monuments, castles, viewpoints and attractions; memorial plaques are left out), ski lifts, fixed speed cameras labelled with their limit, and similar, each with the zoom from which it appears. A place mapped both as a point and as its building is kept once. The offline map draws them as coloured SF Symbol badges in the manner of Apple Maps, with route lines beneath the labels. Over the satellite view the app uses Apple's hybrid imagery with Apple's own labels and points of interest. The satellite map stays loaded after first use; before each switch it is aligned to the offline map's exact view and given time to render, so switching maps is a crossfade between identical views, and returning restores the offline view exactly.

**Corridor maps** cover a long journey across oblasts without loading them whole. `tools/build_corridor.py local-<name> "<Name>" --source <extract.pbf> … --via LAT,LON[,RADIUS_KM] … [--buffer 3] [--box S,W,N,E] [--tracks S,W,N,E]` plans a rough route on the main roads of all sources through the waypoints in order, then keeps every road within the buffer of it, circles around waypoints given a radius (city bypasses and stops) and whole boxes (a destination city). It writes the usual graph, display, search and label files plus `local-<name>-region.json`, which makes the app list the map under **Map**. A 667 km corridor across four oblasts with a 3 km buffer and a whole city came to a 93 MB graph (445,000 directed edges), comparable to the Lviv region, and built in about three minutes. Corridor ids must start with `local-`: they describe someone's journey, so Git ignores them and the privacy check refuses them.

Search indexes (`kyiv-search.json`, `lviv-search.json`) are built by `tools/build_search.py kyiv|lviv` from the same OSM extracts (Kyiv uses the openstreetmap.fr `kiev` extract) and the region's graph: settlements and districts, plus named streets clustered within 1.5 km so each village's "Shevchenka" is its own entry, with `name:en` and the national transliteration. Fuel stations are included too (301 in Kyiv, 380 in the Lviv region), findable by name or brand, with the nearest settlement as context.

**Places from other maps.** `MapLink` (Core, offline) reads a place from shared or pasted text: a Google Maps place pin (`!3d…!4d…`, preferred over the map centre `@lat,lon`), `q=`/`ll=`/`query=`/`daddr=` parameters, `/maps/search|place|dir/lat,lon`, Apple Maps `ll=`, OpenStreetMap `mlat`/`mlon` and `#map=`, `geo:` URIs, links nested in consent or redirect pages (percent-decoded up to three times), degrees-minutes-seconds and bare decimal coordinates (at least three decimals, so phone numbers and prices do not match). A short link (maps.app.goo.gl) carries no coordinates, so `SharedPlaceResolver` opens it once with a 12-second timeout, reads the final address or the page, and as a last resort looks the place's name up with Apple Maps; only that link and name leave the phone. The **GPSLessShare** share extension resolves what Google Maps or another app shares and opens the app with `gpsless://place?lat=…&lon=…&name=…`; where iOS does not let an extension open its app it copies the coordinates for **Paste** in search instead. The app uses the place like a search result: the point being chosen goes to the nearest road within 400 m, and a place outside the loaded map is refused.

`tools/download_kyiv.py` fetches a bounded Overpass extract. `tools/build_kyiv.py` creates the directed graph, map GeoJSON, manifest and local font glyphs. Both builders share `tools/osm_graph.py`; rebuilding Kyiv from its saved extract reproduces the bundled files byte for byte. These scripts run on the Mac, not in the app. The original bulk response stays in ignored `data-source/`. Regenerating data changes the snapshot and intentionally prevents replay against a mismatched road graph.

## Recordings and replay

Drives are streamed into standard **gzip-compressed JSON Lines** files (`.jsonl.gz`), accessible at **Files → On My iPhone → GPSLess → Drives**. Small reference files remain plain `.jsonl` in **FieldReferences**. Both folders are also available through Finder file sharing or Xcode's Devices and Simulators → Download Container. Keep both folders together when recovering a test. App updates preserve the files; deleting the app removes its local data. Nothing is uploaded or automatically deleted. Swipe a file in Recorded drives to delete it explicitly.

The one-minute noisy-data benchmark measured **1.72 MB/minute**, approximately **103 MB/hour**, including all requested sensor rates, position updates, camera diagnostics and 24 road hypotheses per diagnostic snapshot. This is a representative benchmark, not a guaranteed rate on every device. Live **REC** and Recorded drives display actual compressed file sizes. Compression preserves all stored numeric precision.

Each start/resume creates a new segment, including calibration before movement. Format **4** requires the selected route in its header; the streams otherwise retain format 3's layout:

| Record | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| `header` | Selected route's ordered directed edges and A/B positions, session UUID, wall clock and uptime, starting road/direction/uncertainty, deterministic seed, map snapshot, hardware model, OS/app/engine versions, sensor availability, requested rates, mount/axis/unit conventions, previous segment filename, SHA-256 of estimator/service sources and road graph |
| `raw` | Approximately 100 Hz Core Motion user acceleration in g, rotation in rad/s, gravity, attitude quaternion x/y/z/w, calibrated magnetic vector and accuracy flag |
| `sensor` | Independent accelerometer including gravity and gyroscope at requested 100 Hz; raw magnetic vector at requested 5 Hz; relative altitude and pressure in kPa whenever Core Motion provides them |
| `calibration` | Accepted interval/count, forward/lateral/yaw biases, forward and yaw standard deviations, pitch and roll; rejected intervals generate `calibration-restarted` events. Since 0.4.2 records include the parked vibration baseline, and a drive that reuses the app's calibration starts with one (reason `reused`) carrying the current gravity |
| `motion-processing` | Approximately 1 Hz propagated gravity, residual three-axis gyro bias, gravity-direction disagreement with Core Motion, difference in forward acceleration and age of the stationary calibration |
| `sample` | Approximately 20 Hz bias-corrected inputs actually consumed by the estimator; acceleration in m/s², angles in radians, sensor time in seconds since boot |
| `estimate` / `decision` | Position, heading, speed, uncertainty, continuous road-path probability mass (since engine 1.4), turn matches, alternative paths and reset reason; state/anchor transitions retained explicitly |
| `road-update` | Approximately 5 Hz predicted position before the current road-evidence weighting, previous published estimate, resulting estimate, east/north adjustment and magnitude, heading residual; optional representative `curvatureEvidence` with observation interval, start/end candidate positions, measured/mapped heading changes, residual, sigma, lane-motion tolerance and weight multiplier; optional `landmarkCorrection` with signal ID, timed junction, observation error, actual position/speed/bias changes, posterior spread and calibration interval distance/duration |
| `road-signal` | Shared signal ID across detected/accepted/rejected events, observation interval, start road position, measured angle, mapped angle, residual, reason and associated before/after road update |
| `route-constraint` | Legacy engine 2.1 route-gate rows (those builds only): expected route-bend offsets and angle, observed yaw, affected probability, speed before/after the constraint and waiting time |
| `wheelbase-calibration` | Each interval between matched turn midpoints: mapped and vibration distance, wheelbase used, resulting estimate and its uncertainty, pooled estimate, or the rejection reason. The header records the prior evidence (`wheelbaseEvidence…`) and whether it was applied |
| `vibration-speed` | Every 0.5 s while calibrated: vibration speed and its standard deviation, parked probability, residual acceleration bias, axle-echo strength, rolling vibration above the parked reference and the wheelbase used; replay applies these rows |
| `engine-state` | Approximately 1 Hz particle count, effective population, stopped state, unanchored time, heading mismatch duration, integrated turn and top 24 weighted edge hypotheses with estimated sensor biases; these remain edge-based for detailed inspection even when the display aggregates continuous paths |
| `stop-correction` | One event per automatic stop: estimated low-speed onset, before/after road positions, removed metres and applied/skipped reason; separate from road-signal corrections |
| `speed-correction` | Each road-bump speed measurement that differed from the inertial speed by 5 km/h or more at the start of a disagreement (at most every 5 s): time, speed before, measured speed and the running count |
| `visual-speed` | Legacy camera builds only: at most 5 Hz frame timing, selected region, measured lens height, focal lengths, feature/inlier counts, texture, attitude disagreement, estimated speed/uncertainty/quality, acceptance/rejection reason and actual velocity effect; no image data |
| `gps-reference` | Optional reference-only location/speed/course/altitude and their accuracy, precise acquisition/receipt clocks, age, source/quality flags and batch size; at most 1 Hz; no estimator input |
| `gps-trace-event` | GPS collection lifecycle, authorization/accuracy permission, location errors, observed delivery gaps, trace UUID and retained/throttled counts |
| `context` | Periodic wall-time/uptime correlation, battery, thermal state, low-power mode, lifecycle and pause events; battery level -1 means unavailable |
| `sensor-gap` / `sensor-error` | Missing motion intervals and auxiliary sensor errors |
| `footer` | Finish reason, record counts and compressed/uncompressed bytes before footer |
| `field-reference` | Legacy builds only (the manual reference flow is removed; GPS tracing replaces it): marked time and selected road coordinate, previous estimate with its own timestamp, associated segment, landmark note, optional odometer, map and build identity |

Every drive row has a sequence number and callback-receipt uptime. Keep original sensor timestamps for integration; receipt time helps diagnose scheduling delays. Requested rates are not guaranteed delivery rates. A changed phone clock can be detected from periodic clock/uptime pairs. The reference is stored in a separate atomic file even if another drive is never started. Manual position resets retain the previous estimate for comparison; confirm the corrected road point before saving or starting again.

Buffers flush at 64 KB or after two seconds of incoming data, including a gzip synchronization boundary; the file is synchronized to storage roughly every five seconds and on pause. A process termination may lose a partial tail, which replay discards while preserving complete rows even without a finished gzip trailer. There is no fixed drive-size cutoff. When less than 128 MB of available storage is reported, recording stops and the UI shows **REC ERROR** while positioning continues. Storage needs depend on delivered rates and drive length; export and clear old tests as needed. No images/video, microphone, contact, cellular or Wi-Fi data is recorded.

Replay incrementally decompresses and consumes processed `sample` rows, explicit `event` / `confirmed-stop` observations and accepted `visual-speed` observations, recalculates them with the **currently installed engine**, and displays the reconstructed trail and final estimate. GPS reference and GPS event rows are always ignored. Calibration-restart diagnostics may contain a sample but are excluded from replay physics. Raw streams allow different calibration models to be evaluated separately later; replay does not yet rerun raw-sensor calibration. Older recordings retain their original processed acceleration signs, so ordinary replay of an engine 1.1/1.2 departure does not evaluate the corrected sensor conversion; that requires separate reprocessing. Recorded estimates and field references are never fed back as observations. The original output remains in the recording for comparison. Formats 2, 3 and 4 are readable; the map snapshot and recorded map hash must match. Format 4 also requires a valid route containing the starting edge; replay uses that recorded route. Source and map hashes distinguish exactly which local implementation generated a drive. After replay, manual position and route selection are required before driving.

To evaluate the new gravity processing on an old recording, use the Mac raw replay tool, which runs the same processor as the live app. The duration limit is measured from the first raw frame, including calibration. Its output is a diagnostic replay and supplies no independent field truth:

```sh
swiftc -O Core/*.swift tools/replay_raw/main.swift -o build/replay-raw
build/replay-raw GPSLess/OfflineData/kyiv-graph.json /path/to/drive.jsonl.gz build/raw-replay.jsonl 180
```

Engine 3.0 recordings store the wheelbase in the header (`wheelbaseMetres`). For older recordings pass the car's value, for example `build/replay-raw --wheelbase=2.75 …`; otherwise the 2.70 m default applies.

A drive started without a new parked calibration (header `startupCalibration` = `reused-from-current-app-session`) continues the processor state of the previous drive. The raw replay resumes from its `reused` calibration row; recordings before 0.4.2 lack that row, so the tool first runs the previous segments named in `previousRecording` through the processor, which must lie in the same folder. Without them it waits for a parked calibration and replays only the rest of the drive from the recorded starting point: on one such drive the replayed position ended 1.1 km off, while the phone had tracked it within 15 m. The summary line states which calibration was used.

New calibration records include the applied gyro-offset vector, measured unfiltered gyro mean, standard error in vehicle right/up/forward axes, gravity reference, method and reason. Older records without the new optional diagnostics remain readable. A confirmed-stop event records whether that input reference changed; ordinary sample replay clears the engine's old residuals at the same event without rerunning raw calibration.

To inspect exported turn recognition and corrections on the Mac, run `python3 tools/inspect_drive.py /path/to/exported-folder --last 30`. It streams files, reports actual storage rate and event counts, and prints observed versus mapped angles, acceptance/rejection reasons, before/after coordinates, road-evidence adjustment, uncertainty and anchor count. An accepted signal may primarily reduce uncertainty; the log does not claim that every match causes a large position jump. The before-evidence point is the weighted prediction over connected road hypotheses for that filter update, not an independent GPS fix or a second unconstrained navigation system. The full motion history remains available for deeper analysis.

## Simulation Lab and 3D replay

The Mac-only environment in `tools/simulation` generates autonomous drives on the bundled Kyiv road graph and runs the **same Swift Core sources** as the phone. It does not change the iPhone app or feed the estimator GPS, intended routes, simulated positions or speeds. The bridge checks legal route transitions as an input validation step; the tracking engine receives only the initial road position, map and motion stream.

```sh
bash tools/simulation/setup.sh
tools/simulation/.venv/bin/python tools/simulation/sim.py prepare --recording /absolute/path/to/drive.jsonl.gz
tools/simulation/.venv/bin/python tools/simulation/sim.py serve
```

Open **http://127.0.0.1:8940**. `prepare` imports the specified recording, fits short-term sensor noise, reconstructs its first 160 driving seconds, and generates ideal, nominal and stress-control runs. Without a recording, use `sim.py run` to create synthetic runs. Installation needs Python 3.11 or later, Node/npm, Git and the Xcode Swift compiler; dependencies download once, and replay uses locally served Three.js assets. The tested environment is Python 3.14, Node 20 and Xcode 26.3.

- **Drive** gives a third-person chase camera, a detailed vehicle with rotating/steering wheels, shadows and illustrative city scenery. **Cinema** hides the diagnostic UI and overlays for a clean presentation, retaining a simulated-drive label. Kyiv road geometry is real map data; buildings, trees, road widths and vehicle appearance are illustrative, not reconstructed Kyiv imagery.
- **Fit route**, **Top** and **Follow** provide diagnostic cameras. Mint shows synthetic truth or the original recorded engine, amber shows the current engine, and purple marks accepted turn decisions. The uncertainty ring retains its real scale; car symbols enlarge in overview mode.
- Play, pause, scrub, change speed, click a chart or a road-signal event to seek. Space toggles playback and arrow keys step one second. Charts show motion input, yaw, error, speed and per-update road adjustments; details retain rejected signals and road hypotheses. A turn event's adjustment is that update's displacement, not the total accumulated correction over the manoeuvre.
- **New simulation** selects a Kyiv starting area or explicit directed edge, random seed, route length, cruise limit, sensor profile and initial offset. **Run 6-trial suite** runs two routes with ideal/nominal/stress profiles. Results and failures are saved for inspection. The CLI supports longer bounded batches: `sim.py batch --count 12 --seed 100 --length 1000`.

The motion model rounds centre-line corners, plans curvature-limited speed, acceleration, braking and a stop, and computes mounted-phone specific force and rotation at 100 Hz. It includes dashboard lever-arm acceleration, a causal low-order pitch/roll response, synthetic road height, vibration, scale error, correlated sensor bias, measurement noise, jitter and optional sample loss. Pinned [ACEINNA gnss-ins-sim](https://github.com/Aceinna/gnss-ins-sim/tree/966ff271bf21d08fbd0e31d1eb47fa5f3acef8d3) supplies accelerometer/gyro error generation. NumPy and SciPy versions are pinned. A separate approximate gravity-separation model produces Core Motion-shaped measurements; **it does not reproduce Apple's private fusion implementation**. The Swift bridge uses the same `VehicleMotionProcessor` as the service for calibration, gravity propagation and averaging. Dedicated processor tests also inject acceleration-correlated gravity leakage, gyro offsets and temperature-like drift rather than relying only on the simulator's independent tilt noise.

Field replay keeps the original live estimates/events separate from the current engine replay. Neither is independent field truth. The paired simulation infers a connected route and sparse distance timeline from recorded estimates, then retimes it to avoid physically implausible motion caused by estimator jumps. Sensor overlays explicitly align recorded time by that inferred distance; the comparison is of shapes and statistics, not exact driving timing. Only the first 160 seconds precede the known U-turn in the current valid test. The field-inspired profile fits short-term noise and mounting angles from stationary data and the 2–45 Hz acceleration-vibration spectrum from driving data, subtracting the fitted white-noise floor. Spectral synthesis generates the whole drive without repeating a short block. This is an effective model of car, phone and Core Motion output together. Four seconds of stationary data cannot establish long-term bias stability or distinguish every vibration/fusion effect. Drift, gravity errors and vehicle parameters remain assumptions. A recording used to fit the simulator is not independent validation data.

All artifacts stay in ignored `build/simulation`: compressed 100 Hz synthetic measurements, processed engine rows/corrections, full-rate truth arrays, scenario parameters and browser replays. Each run includes map, native-engine and simulator source hashes, sensor-library commit and random seeds. The native binary is rebuilt when Core sources change; restart the server after changing simulator Python sources. Runs are limited to 20 minutes, UI batches to 12 trials, and new UI jobs are refused after saved runs exceed 2 GB. No originals are edited or automatically deleted. The loopback server serves only its viewer and run artifacts and accepts same-origin simulation requests; it does not expose the repository or upload recordings.

This environment supports autonomous testing and comparison across engine revisions. It does **not** automatically rewrite or promote positioning-engine changes. Simulation accuracy scores apply to the synthetic trajectory, including error accumulated after a tracking failure, and do not establish accuracy on Ukrainian roads.

```sh
tools/simulation/.venv/bin/python -m unittest discover -s tools/simulation -p 'test_*.py' -v
swift test
```

## Verification

`python3 tools/privacy_check.py` checks everything Git would publish for recordings, personal paths, team IDs, unexpected email addresses, strings listed in the ignored `Config/private-patterns.txt`, and coordinates or road IDs near where your own local recordings start, end or pass. The hooks in `.githooks` run it on commit and push (`git config core.hooksPath .githooks`).

Run the engine/graph and recording checks with `swift test`. They cover road snapping and dragging, parallel-road isolation, standstill, constant-speed driving, a right turn, noisy curves, U-turns, accepted and rejected road-signal events, sensor gaps, phone handling, uncertainty after stopping, deterministic replay with diagnostics enabled, loading the real Kyiv graph, actual file persistence across multiple buffers, record counts/timestamps, standard gzip validation, compressed storage budget, truncated compressed/plain tails, corrupt interior rows, version 2 compatibility and independently saved field references. `RouteEvidenceTrackingTests` check that a sequence of uneven ±40° bends identifies each bend within 15 m although the estimate starts 250 m ahead, that the heading profile of a 700 m sweeping bend moves an estimate 200 m past it back to within 25 m, that the estimate waits at a right angle until the gyro turns, and that speed corrections are counted once per disagreement. `VehicleSpeedObserverTests` include a 120 km/h cruise, which the 0.1 s delay limit read as 59 km/h.

Continuous-road regression checks compare identical straight geometry divided into different edge lengths, ensure passing side roads does not suppress forward progress, and keep competing forks, prohibited continuations and parallel roads separate. The Xcode UI test covers map loading, road direction, actual marker dragging, mounting instructions, simulator sensor-unavailable recovery, saving a reference and the export controls. Test the production sensor path only on a physical iPhone. Field validation should compare the saved output to timestamped landmark observations and odometer distances, never feed those references into the estimator. Long straight roads and similar neighbouring junctions remain fundamental limitations of phone-only inertial tracking.

Curvature checks cover continuous tangent equivalence across differently split polylines, straight seams without curvature evidence, both signs of gentle S-bends, lane changes without turn anchors, and recording compatibility. A recorded curvature window describes one representative weighted hypothesis, not an independently confirmed correction. Particle windows can have different start times after resampling; diagnostic window counts are not counts of physical bends. Recording rates remain unchanged.

## Camera assistance (removed)

Rear-camera speed assistance (engines 1.8–3.0, off by default) is removed from the app; road-bump vibration speed replaced it. Recorded `visual-speed` rows still replay, and the estimator code that consumes them remains in Core for that purpose.
